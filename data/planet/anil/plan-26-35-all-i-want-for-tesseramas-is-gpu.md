<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#a-year-of-tessera-embeddings" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>A year of Tessera embeddings</h2>
<p>It's been a whole year since the first GeoTessera release, woohoo! I <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/geotessera-a-year-on">put together a brief recap</a> and released <a href="https://github.com/ucam-eo/geotessera/releases/tag/v0.10.0">GeoTessera 0.10</a>, which moves our hosting over to the <a href="https://source.coop/">Source Cooperative</a> with UTM-native Zarr routing and Matryoshka reads for the v2 beta.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~avsm2/embedding_requests_map.html"> <img src="https://anil.recoil.org/images/embedding_requests_map.gif" alt="%c" title="A year of public TESSERA embedding requests, in the order they were filed"> </a></p>
<p>The sync of the <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/tessera-v11-out">Tessera v1.1 embeddings</a> onto the Source Cooperative also finished this week, so those are now browsable via <a href="https://tze.geotessera.org">tze.geotessera.org</a> as well (click on the model version on the top right). The full global run for v1.1 for 2017-2025 has unfortunately been delayed by a month due to GPU shortages. If you can help us get access to more GPUs then please get in touch. Smaller cards are fine, since we can parallelise the inference across many of them per tile. <a href="https://www.tunbury.org/">Mark Elvers</a> has <a href="https://www.tunbury.org/2026/08/31/week-35-2025/">mostly finished the v2 inference ablations</a>, so compute is now what we're short of.</p>
<p><a href="https://tze.geotessera.org/?store=v1.1"> <img src="https://anil.recoil.org/images/tze-explorer-v1.1-ss-1.webp" alt="%c" title="The green false colour shows the expanded v1.1 coastal tiles, which now capture all of the UK including islands"> </a></p>
<p>Relatedly, I read a nice paper on the <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27175">temporal sensitivity of Tessera embeddings</a>. The authors keep the Tessera encoder frozen and recompute its embeddings over successively shorter observation windows, from a full year (which is what we generate by default) right down to a single day (!). They then benchmark linear probes and UNet heads against from-scratch networks on various datasets.</p>
<p>Interestingly, accuracy degrades gradually rather than collapsing. Contracting the temporal window from a year down to a month drops 39% of the segmentation accuracy on the phenology-driven PASTIS-R crop task. I was surprised to see that even single-day embeddings classify land cover at 3.4 times the background chance baseline, so temporal coverage seems practical to tune if you're willing to pay the inference cost.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>The benefit of using embeddings is more evident when labels are scarce: with
only 1% of the labels TESSERA-UNet already achieves ∼24 mIoU on PASTIS-R,
some 50% above the best from-scratch UNet, and even the linear probe matches
or exceeds every fully supervised from-scratch UNet there, while on DEN the
embedding-based models stay essentially flat from 1% of the labels onwards
and the from-scratch UNets only catch up between 30% and 100%, so the models
only draw level on DEN at saturation. The embeddings thus pay off whenever
the mapping task is phenology-driven or labels are scarce, and lose their
edge only when it is spectral and labels are abundant, a combination rarely
met in operational LULC production.
<cite>-- <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27175">Temporal Sensitivity Analysis of Tessera Embeddings</a>, Julia Guerrero-Viu et al, Aug 2026</cite></p>
</blockquote>

<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#scrutineer-scanning-with-a-local-glm" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>Scrutineer scanning with a local GLM</h2>
<p>My <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/rumour-is-the-exploit">musings on security embargoes</a> were covered pretty widely online, with discussions on <a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49480466">HN</a> and <a href="https://lobste.rs/s/t73wqi/just_rumour_bug_is_enough_find_security">Lobsters</a>. I loved seeing <a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49480777">Nick Craig-Wood participate</a>, as he maintains <a href="https://rclone.org/">rclone</a> which we <a href="https://www.tunbury.org/2026/01/06/ceph-notes/#copying-data">use extensively to manage filesync in the EEG</a>. The rclone project fielded around 20 security disclosures in its first decade, and now 40+ in the last month alone. He's now considering merging security fixes straight to trunk, rather than holding embargoed branches. The Lobsters thread also pointed me at the <a href="https://zerodayclock.com/">Zero Day Clock</a>, which is a depressingly incredible graph.</p>
<p><a href="https://zerodayclock.com/"> <img src="https://anil.recoil.org/images/zeroday-clock.webp" alt="%c" title="The Zero day clock to exploitation"> </a></p>
<p>As a followup, I deployed <a href="https://nesbitt.io">Andrew Nesbitt</a>'s Scrutineer to scan my own OCaml code, but driven by GLM 5.3 running on my Mac Studio rather than a commercial code model. I wrote up quick <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/scrutineer-local-llm">notes on the deployment</a>. The short version is it's surfacing hundreds of potential findings to triage, so there's some work ahead. <a href="https://github.com/samoht">Thomas Gazagnaire</a> is giving me a hand though!</p>
<p><img src="https://anil.recoil.org/images/scrutineer-ss-5.webp" alt="%c" title="Scrutineer's triage view of an example harmless finding in cohttp"></p>
<p>To support this, I refreshed my <a href="https://tangled.org/anil.recoil.org/ocaml-deepseek">ocaml-deepseek</a> inference engine, which I first built for <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/language-integrated-llms">language-integrated LLMs</a> but am now exposing as a server. It is now renamed <code>ds4</code> to match the upstream project, and the <code>glm-5.3</code> branch serves GLM 5.3 Flash over an OpenAI-compatible endpoint for Scrutineer to use.</p>

<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#tdm-reservations-for-evidence-tap" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>TDM reservations for Evidence TAP</h2>
<p>The Evidence TAP database is up to ~20 million fulltexts now, thanks to an import from Semantic Scholar as well as the download engine continuing to get education and conservation fulltexts.</p>
<p>I also published <a href="https://tangled.org/anil.recoil.org/ocaml-tdmrep">ocaml-tdmrep</a>, an OCaml implementation of the W3C <a href="https://www.w3.org/community/reports/tdmrep/CG-FINAL-tdmrep-20240202/">TDM Reservation Protocol</a>, to help ensure the literature downloader respects publishers' machine-readable text-and-data-mining reservations while crawling open access versions.</p>
