<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#a-first-year-viva-in-planetary-law" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>A first-year viva in planetary law</h2>
<p>Something new for me was examining a first year PhD viva in the Faculty of Law, as I continue my departmental bingo around the University. The candidate was <a href="https://www.law.cam.ac.uk/people/research-students/ys-sun/90662">Yirong Sun</a>, supervised by <a href="https://www.jennifercobbe.com/">Jennifer Cobbe</a>, and the other examiner (thankfully from Law) was <a href="https://www.law.cam.ac.uk/people/academic/hm-grosse-ruse-khan/5328">Professor Henning Grosse Ruse-Khan</a>. I learnt a great deal from the discussion.</p>
<p>Yirong's thesis was an absolutely <em>fascinating</em> argument about the regulation of planetary infrastructures, which covers remote sensing satellites, space situational awareness and space weather monitoring. It sits close to our own work on <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/projects/tessera">TESSERA</a> and <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/projects/plancomp">planetary computing</a>, just seen through the lens of global law rather than computer systems. Her earlier article on this topic before she started her PhD was with Benedict Kingsbury, <a href="https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/cjil/vol26/iss1/4/">Satellite Infrastructures and Law in the Making of Planetary Knowledge</a>, which traces how satellite data became infrastructural to the way we understand the planet.</p>
<p>Our conversation ranged over the commons, and I learnt about adjacent ideas such as <a href="https://www.fao.org/plant-treaty/areas-of-work/the-multilateral-system/smta/en/">standard material transfer agreements</a>, the uniform contracts used to share plant genetic material under one agreed set of benefit sharing terms. I can't comment on how the viva went of course, but I'm sure my enthusiasm for her work is shining through here as I'm keen to continue to learn more!</p>
<p>I've been wondering for ages how we're going to avoid a tragedy of the commons in low earth orbit, since a staggering 1.7 million LEO satellites are <a href="https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso2607/">proposed by 2030</a>! It seems too fast a launch rate for this to work as a <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/cambridge-green-blue">common pool resource</a>, but Henning pointed out during our discussions that there is precedent for international law for deep sea mining via the <a href="https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/energysource/us-ratification-of-the-ocean-treaty-will-unlock-deep-sea-mining/">UN Convention of the Law of the Sea</a>.</p>

<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#downscaling-the-weather-with-tessera" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>Downscaling the weather with TESSERA</h2>
<p>After this excitement at the start of the week, the NVIDIA team visited us again in Cambridge due to <a href="https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=TfyToUUAAAAJ&amp;hl=en">Mike Pritchard</a> being in town. Mike is their director of Climate Simulation Research, and so <a href="https://toao.com">Sadiq Jaffer</a>, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/pedro-marques-sousa">Pedro Sousa</a> and I met with them to compare notes on our various projects, not least the weather downscaling below.</p>
<p><img src="https://anil.recoil.org/images/nvidia-visit-cam-1.webp" alt="%rc">
Mike showed us some really cool projects they're working on, such as
<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31248">StormScale</a> which can simulate storms across
the whole planet at 50x better energy efficiency than the SCREAM physics model. Mike's a former professor from UC Irvine and also gave me plenty of background reading: in particular, this book by David Randall on "<a href="https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691143750/atmosphere-clouds-and-climate">Atmosphere, Clouds, and Climate</a>" is now on my reading list.</p>
<p>On our end, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/pedro-marques-sousa">Pedro Sousa</a> has been leading work on <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/ideas/tessera-weather-downscaling">downscaling weather using TESSERA</a>, and the results are looking exciting. The setup is that weather reanalyses and forecasts come on grids of around 25km, but almost every decision anyone wants to make with them (such as where to site a turbine, or when to cut grass, or whether a road will ice up) happens at a smaller local point rather than a large km-scale grid cell.</p>
<p>The current downscaling approach is to inject that missing sub-grid signal via a hand-crafted topographic descriptor (using things like elevation). Pedro's hypothesis is that a frozen <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/projects/tessera">TESSERA</a> embedding does better, since it encodes land cover, vegetation, water and built surfaces alongside the terrain in one pre-trained model. We'll have a preprint out soon about this, but the TL;DR is that it does! Pedro is heading over to Berkeley and Duke next week, initially for the <a href="https://dl4sci-school.lbl.gov/">Deep Learning for Science</a> summer school, so if you're in the region and want to learn more then find him in Berkeley or Stanford for the next few weeks!</p>
<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#the-infamous-cambridge-weather-station" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>The (in)famous Cambridge weather station</h2>
<p>All this talk of station observations made me wonder a few months ago about the state of our own <a href="https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/dtg/weather/">rooftop weather station</a>, which has been recording Cambridge weather since June 1995. I haven't been up on the roof since about 2004 when I helped rewire the <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/papers/netgames04-ctf">Active BAT location system</a>, so I asked on our local mailing list to enquire if I could help out with it.</p>
<p>The TL;DR is that it's in dire need of fresh hands. The rainfall sensor was fixed after a gap in September 2025, but since a power outage in January this year, the pressure, humidity and wind sensors have all been returning incorrect data, and some components need to be replaced.
<img src="https://anil.recoil.org/images/cl-weather-sensor.webp" alt="%rc" title="The view from the Computer Lab weather station (image credit: James Sharkey)">
The slightly longer story from James Sharkey:</p>
<blockquote>
<p>However, the core issue is that the MetLog hardware that drives most of the sensors is ancient and unmaintained, and most of the physical sensors on the roof have corroded beyond hope of repair.
Probably many of the sensors need complete replacements, but if so, the ancient MetLog device the existing ones are connected to is not a sensible host. So the need is for both money to buy most of a new weather station, and people willing to rewrite lots if not all of the configuration and scripting around the data collection if the MetLog is replaced.</p>
<p>Some of the hardware is nearly as old as I am, although the laser rainfall sensor and temperature probes have been updated much more recently. Incredibly, the company that the MetLog part of the weather station was purchased from in ~1996 is still <a href="https://instromet.co.uk">selling weather stations</a> and responded helpfully when we last spoke to them asking for a datasheet to debug some wiring issues.
<cite>-- James Sharkey, cl-cooler, 2026</cite></p>
</blockquote>
<p><a href="https://quentinsf.com/">Q</a> took on modernising the software some years ago. Unfortunately the old Perl code had been running for over two decades so any changes he made would alter the decades long weather record if it skewed the sampling. Maintaining this stream therefore requires 'shuffling' components into place with minimum downtime (thankfully over an XML-RPC interface) and write text files/SQL in exactly the way the old code had (quirks included).</p>
<p>Some of the sensors in there can be polled, but others simply stream over a serial port and needed something listening continuously on the other end.  It might be time to have buffering serial port proxies that can also rebroadcast! <a href="https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~mgk25/">Markus Kuhn</a> did wonder whether it was really an equipment limitation, or just the old site code holding a connection open permanently and hogging the serial port.</p>
<p>Given how corroded the sensors are, when I'm back in Cambridge in August I'll have a go at some self-hosted <a href="https://www.reddit.com/r/selfhosted/comments/1roxxz2/weather_station_compatible_with_selfhosted/">Ecowitt gear</a> and see how far I get...</p>
<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#moving-tessera-onto-source-cooperative" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>Moving TESSERA onto Source Cooperative</h2>
<p>We have been moving TESSERA's embeddings over to the <a href="https://source.coop">Source Cooperative</a>, following the enthusiastic reaction at <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/cng-london-2026">CNG London last month</a>. The actual mechanics of this are super fiddly, in order to avoid getting stuck with massive egress bandwidth charges. There are terabytes of v1.0 and 1.1 embeddings sitting in an AWS bucket in us-west-2, and the egress costs to pull them all out naively would be well over fifty thousand dollars since there's a HTTP proxy preventing direct transfer over to us-east-2 (to Source Coop). So instead we are juggling the embeddings via Cambridge to avoid the worst of the charges; this should take no more than a week or two I hope!</p>
<p>We now have several model versions to look after, with 1.0, the <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/tessera-v11-out">1.1 we released a few months ago</a>, and the <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/papers/2026-tessera-v2">forthcoming 2.0</a> with its variations in the student models. Source Cooperative is fronted by Cloudflare and so looks like a fantastic home for all of this; Isaac Corley has been <em>really</em> helpful in getting us started on all this. A good deal of my time over the next few months will go into this migration, and also doing the <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/tessera-zarr-v3-layout">conversion to Zarr v3</a> to make that the default access mechanism for every consumer.</p>

<h2><a href="https://anil.recoil.org/news.xml#more-eio-platform-hacking" class="anchor" aria-hidden="true"></a>More Eio platform hacking</h2>
<p>My <a href="https://anil.recoil.org/notes/2026w28">Eio push</a> of the past couple of weeks hit a satisfying milestone as my <a href="https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/pull/850">ftruncate and fdatasync PR</a> was merged by <a href="https://roscidus.com">Thomas Leonard</a> after much cleanup. It moves fsync, shutdown and the socket creation calls (socket, bind and listen) all over to <code>io_uring</code>, which needed the newer liburing 2.15 that I bumped the minimum version to last week. This is now using pretty cutting edge kernel interfaces, which is I think necessary if using uring (Eio supports falling back to normal POSIX with an environment variable if you run into any bugs).</p>
<p>I've also got some PRs for a <a href="https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/pull/875">pre-connect socket options interface</a>, which revises earlier work by <a href="https://github.com/art-w">Arthur Wendling</a>. This one's a little tricky since I'm trying to avoid exposing the low-level sockets interface. Minor PRs include exposing the <a href="https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/pull/860">SO_TYPE query</a> into the portable socket interface. <a href="https://roscidus.com">Thomas Leonard</a> meanwhile factored the Unix error wrapping that was duplicated across the three backends (POSIX, Linux, Windows) into a common <a href="https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/pull/887">Eio_unix.Err module</a>, and added <a href="https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/pull/890">TCP keepalive support on macOS</a> which helps with the OCaml capnp-rpc library.</p>
<p>I'm still down in the weeds with Windows; I have lots of local patches but nothing I'm satisfied with submitting upstream just yet. I also had to do some macOS spelunking since it turns out that <code>poll()</code> on macOS returns <code>POLLNVAL</code> for some device files such as <code>/dev/tty</code> and <code>/dev/null</code>, so I proposed <a href="https://github.com/ocaml-multicore/eio/pull/893">falling back to select() for those descriptors</a>, which is the least invasive fix we could find that leaves the other platforms alone. Interestingly, I mentioned this platform quirkiness to <a href="https://simon.peytonjones.org/">Simon Peyton Jones</a> who sent me off to the <a href="https://gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/ghc/-/work_items/27519">Haskell bug tracker</a> where they are running into <a href="https://gitlab.haskell.org/ghc/ghc/-/work_items/22588#note_686178">very similar issues</a> to us.</p>
